summary: "Sole engineer on the Unity and Ultraleap rebuild of TrAIT, a gamified stroke-rehabilitation task whose earlier Kinect version was validated in a peer-reviewed trial."
context: "TrAIT turns upper-limb stroke rehabilitation into a game, because conventional therapy sessions produce few therapeutic reaches and motor relearning needs many more. The original version ran on a Microsoft Kinect and was evaluated in a published trial (Neurorehabilitation and Neural Repair, 2024). I was the sole engineer on its rebuild in Unity with Ultraleap hand tracking, the software the research team updated for its follow-up work."
decision: "I structured the app as a small service architecture (a game manager with core services and about twenty scene controllers) so calibration, gameplay, telemetry and export could change independently. Ultraleap tracking drives a motion tracker with a keyboard fallback for testing; a multi-stage calibration fits each patient's reachable workspace, with protection against bad captures and a redo path. Progression runs over twelve streak-based levels. Every session writes per-tick research telemetry and exports it to the lab by email, and editor tooling turns configuration schemas into generated accessors so the team could adjust content without code."
outcome: "A working rebuild delivered end to end as the only engineer, from calibration to research data export. The trial results belong to the earlier Kinect version, not to this build."
